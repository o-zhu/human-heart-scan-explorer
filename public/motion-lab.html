Settings & comparison controls

Approximate anatomical planes for this specimen; no patient-position registration. Cuts stay in the reference orientation during modeled motion.

The clinical comparison uses the same crop, phase, threshold, camera and lighting for both surfaces. These are intensity isosurfaces; individual chambers are not segmented.

Playback speed is a viewing choice. The source CT does not supply physical phase timestamps.

WHAT THIS TEST ESTABLISHES

Keep the surface.
Move its coordinates.

The modeled animation applies one smooth deformation to each existing vertex. The whole-heart view uses the existing simplified display mesh; the small view uses the archived native reconstruction. Neither is remeshed or geometrically smoothed during animation.

The native region follows the same field in donor coordinates. Its camera follows the moving center so fine folds remain visible. Crop boundaries are open.

What the animation means

The specimen is fixed tissue. Its resting pose is not a known end-diastolic state. This pilot has an assumed long axis and central cavity; it has not been fitted to LV/RV boundaries or registered to SlicerHeart.

The cavity-aware field concentrates volume change near its assumed cavity and approaches volume preservation farther away. It is a geometric demonstration, not a solved cardiac mechanics model. Valves and chordae do not have independent motion or contact.

The deformation is checked for a positive local Jacobian, exact rest recovery and cycle closure. These are numerical checks, not anatomical validation.
