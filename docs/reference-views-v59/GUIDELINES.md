# PLAX mitral illustration: targeted repair guidelines — v59

Reviewed November 12, 2026 (America/Chicago). Scope: **TTE `plax_mv` only**, ASE Table 2.4.

## Why the prior image needed another repair

The v54 drawing contained a false myocardial seal at the mitral annulus. The v58 repair removed that seal but left two weaknesses: most of the visible LA perimeter was only a single contour, and overlapping curves made the LVOT, aortic cusps and aortomitral attachments unclear. A single line can represent a thin wall in some drawing systems; here it conflicted with the finite tissue bands used elsewhere and failed the requested reference standard.

The v58 pass for this image is superseded. Its manifest and pixels are historical evidence. The other 55 illustrations are unchanged by this follow-up; their v58 records are carried over, not represented as newly reviewed.

## Anatomical construction contract

### Imaging plane

Use a mitral-focused parasternal long-axis section. LV extends toward display left; LA lies display right and posterior to the aortic region; LVOT and aortic root extend upper-right. The upper myocardial band is the basal septal/anterior LV boundary, never a free intracavitary spur.

Keep MV and LA dominant. Partial aortic-root context may be cropped at the sector edge. Do not add a full RVOT–PV–PA course: that is the separate ASE 2.5 acquisition. Do not invent a complete true LV apex in this zoom.

### Atrial wall

Draw a thin filled tissue ribbon with two distinguishable interfaces around the LA roof, rightward contour and far-field/posterior contour. It must be appreciably thinner than LV myocardium yet visible after thumbnail reduction.

For this repair, put the rounded LA perimeter entirely inside the gray sector with an ivory margin. The sector is an imaging envelope, not atrial tissue.

The ribbon joins the annular regions at its ends. It must **not** close the atrial side of the mitral opening, become an oval muscle ring, or create an artificial aperture elsewhere. Preserve a thin fibrous relationship beneath the aortic root; do not extend a uniform thick muscular band through the aortomitral curtain.

### Mitral opening and attachments

Show exactly two primary leaflet profiles. The longer anterior leaflet attaches at the anterior mitral hinge in the aortomitral region. The shorter posterior leaflet attaches at the basal inferolateral atrioventricular junction. Each needs an identifiable fixed attachment and free edge; neither should float inside LV.

In this diastolic schematic, both leaflets open into LV and their free edges remain separated. Trace an open blood-space route from LA through the annulus into LV without crossing a myocardial bridge, residual contour, membrane or fused leaflet mass.

Chordae and papillary support are simplified. The icon identifies the view and principal relationships; it does not replace the complete acquisition standard, which also evaluates motion and subvalvular anatomy.

### LVOT

LVOT is the proximal outflow portion of the LV cavity. Its anterior boundary is the basal septum; its posterior boundary is AML and fibrous aortomitral continuity. Trace both borders toward AV.

Do not make LVOT an isolated second tube, blind pouch or additional chamber. Prohibit an internal myocardial stripe, duplicated parallel lumen, septal hook or unexplained pale loop.

The anterior mitral hinge is ventricular to the aortic annulus. A short fibrous curtain connects the distinct hinge regions. AML must not merge directly into a free aortic cusp as one elongated leaflet.

### Aortic root

Show one AV plane and one aortic lumen distal to it. The posterior root wall/fibrous region separates the outflow from LA. No direct Ao–LA communication is permitted.

Aortic continuation may leave the upper-right sector edge as a cropped vessel. This legitimate vessel crop differs from using the sector to replace the rounded LA wall.

Two AV cusp profiles in a longitudinal section do not imply a bicuspid valve; they are section profiles, not a count of all cusps.

### Phase

Use a simplified diastolic-filling state: **MV open, AV closed**. Thin opposed aortic cusp profiles attach to the root and meet centrally.

Trace LV into LVOT **up to** the closed AV. Do not require an unobstructed white path through the closed valve into Ao. Anatomical continuity and instantaneous patency are distinct checks. Thin valve closure is acceptable; a thick muscle cap is not.

Do not infer quantitative function, valve area, excursion or an exact diastolic subphase from this static drawing.

## Visual and label contract

Retain warm ivory background/blood spaces, muted terracotta tissue, thin dark-brown contours, gray sector and bold dark sans-serif labels. No decorative texture, 3D shading, Doppler, ECG, title, screenshot frame or watermark.

| Label | Required location or target |
| --- | --- |
| LV | Left ventricular blood space |
| LA | Atrial blood space |
| MV | Short leader ending on the anterior mitral leaflet |
| LVOT | Proximal outflow blood space before AV, directly or by short leader |
| Ao | Aortic lumen distal to AV |

Keep labels clear of hinges, coaptation and wall interfaces. LV, LA and MV remain dominant. Do not enlarge anatomy to accommodate text.

## Release inspection: judge the pixels

A detailed prompt is a specification, not evidence that generation succeeded.

1. Trace the complete visible LA tissue ribbon and distinguish both interfaces.
2. Trace the open LA–MV–LV route; restoring the wall must not restore the earlier seal.
3. Identify each mitral hinge and free tip independently.
4. Follow both LVOT borders to AV and identify one distal aortic lumen.
5. Distinguish AML hinge, fibrous curtain and aortic cusp hinge.
6. Verify open MV/closed AV and each label target.
7. Inspect the master, actual 960px WebP, actual 384px thumbnail and a 200px rendition of the encoded thumbnail.
8. After publication, inspect the deployed card and enlarged illustration and confirm the new asset version.
9. If a gate fails, record the observed defect, edit the candidate and recheck the entire affected junction.

A roughly 2–3px atrial ribbon at a 200px display is an optional drawing-readability target, not an anatomical measurement. Fine cusp and hinge morphology is judged primarily enlarged.

Further editing should address an observed defect. Every later correction must preserve the open annulus and complete atrial wall together.

## Scope

This review resolves the identified wall and outflow ambiguity in a simplified teaching illustration. It is source-based visual QC, not independent clinician certification, quantitative anatomy, patient correspondence or validation of valve motion. These limitations do not excuse an incorrect chamber connection, missing required boundary or misdirected label.
